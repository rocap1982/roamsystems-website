# ISOFIX Campervan Seating: Safe Travel with Children

Many families dream of hitting the road in a campervan, but travelling with young children raises a crucial question — how do you keep them safe? Standard campervan seating often lacks the safety features found in family cars, leaving parents to make do or compromise. This guide explains what ISOFIX campervan seating means, why it matters, and what to look for when converting your van for family adventures.

## What Is ISOFIX?

ISOFIX is an international standard for attaching child seats directly to a vehicle's chassis, bypassing the seatbelt entirely. The system uses two anchor points built into the seat structure, creating a rigid connection between the child seat and the vehicle frame. This dramatically reduces the risk of the child seat moving during a collision.

In standard cars, ISOFIX points are built into the rear seat structure at the factory. In campervans, it's more complex — your rear seating is usually fitted after conversion, which means ISOFIX points are only present if they've been deliberately engineered into the seat frame.

## Why Campervans Are Different

In a family car, the rear seats are fitted by the vehicle manufacturer. Campervans are different. The rear seating is a conversion — it's added after the base vehicle leaves the factory. Unless the conversion seating has been independently tested, there is no evidence of the strength of its seat belt anchorages.

This is where independent testing matters. ECE Regulation 14 covers seat belt anchorages. The rear travel seat of our U-shape frame has been independently pull tested to the M1 load requirements of ECE R14.09, and its ISOFIX anchorages have been tested to ECE R145. These were static pull tests on a steel test structure. They were not carried out in a vehicle and they are not crash tests. Tell your insurer about any seating you add.

## ISOFIX in Campervan Seating: What to Look For

If you're planning to travel with young children, you need rear seating that provides:

1. **Independent seat belt anchorage testing** — confirms the seat has been pull tested for seat belt anchorage strength to ECE R14.09
2. **Integrated ISOFIX anchor points** — allows you to fit a compatible child seat safely
3. **3-point seatbelts**

The combination of all three is rare in campervan furniture. Most conversion furniture — even well-made, expensive furniture — does not include these features because they require significant engineering and independent testing.

## Fitting a Child Seat in a Campervan: Practical Tips

Even with ISOFIX points in place, there are a few things to check before fitting a child seat:

**Check child seat compatibility.** Not all ISOFIX child seats are compatible with every ISOFIX installation. Check the seat manufacturer's vehicle approval list before purchasing. The ISOFIX connection standardises the anchor point, but the geometry and clearances vary between installations.

**Consider direction of travel.** Rear-facing child seats provide greater protection for infants and toddlers because they spread the force of a collision across the whole back. If your van has headroom and length to accommodate a rear-facing seat, it's worth considering for younger children.

**Check weight limits.** Always refer to the child seat manufacturer's maximum weight limit and age guidance, not just your vehicle's payload. Child seats are rated for specific weight ranges — using the wrong seat for your child's size reduces their effectiveness.

**Secure loose items.** In any sudden stop, unsecured items in a van become projectiles. Make sure storage drawers and lockers have positive latches.
