# Choosing the Right Campervan Kitchen Pod

The kitchen is one of the parts of a campervan conversion that gets used every single trip. Whether it's a morning brew before a hike or a proper meal at the end of a long drive, a well-designed kitchen pod turns a converted van into somewhere you actually want to spend time. Get it wrong, though, and you end up with a setup that's awkward to use, hard to keep tidy, and slowly resented for years.

This guide walks through what to look for in a campervan kitchen pod in the UK — including layout, sizing for SWB vs LWB vans, hob compatibility, materials, and how it fits with the rest of your build.

---

## What Is a Campervan Kitchen Pod?

A kitchen pod is a self-contained unit that combines your hob, sink (in some configurations), worktop, and storage into one piece of furniture that sits inside the van. Instead of building cabinetry into the bodywork, a pod gives you a modular kitchen that can be fitted, removed, or upgraded as a single component.

The main advantage of a pod over fully built-in cabinetry is flexibility — pods can be tailored to your van's wheelbase, your cooking style, and your storage needs without the cost or complexity of a bespoke install. They also tend to make resale easier, since the work isn't permanently fixed to the vehicle.

---

## SWB vs LWB: Sizing the Pod to the Van

The first decision is whether your van is short wheelbase (SWB) or long wheelbase (LWB), because pods are usually built in two sizes to match.

**Short wheelbase (SWB)** vans — the standard VW T5, T6, and T6.1, and the SWB Ford Transit Custom — have a more compact load area. A SWB pod prioritises efficient use of space, with shorter overall length and a layout that doesn't intrude on rear seating or sleeping configurations.

**Long wheelbase (LWB)** vans give you significantly more interior length, which means you can fit a larger pod with more worktop area, additional drawers, and more headroom for taller appliances or accessories.

## Pod Layouts: Static vs Pull-Out

There are two broad layout philosophies in campervan kitchens, and the right one depends on how much you cook and how often.

**Static pods** are fixed units, usually arranged along one side of the van, with the hob, worktop, and storage all in their normal positions. They're stable, robust, and feel more like a real kitchen — you can prep on the worktop while the kettle boils. They take up a permanent footprint, though, which means slightly less floor space when the van isn't being cooked in.

**Pull-out pods** are designed to slide out from the rear of the van when cooking, then push back in when you're on the move. They keep the interior open and uncluttered, and they're great for outdoor cooking — particularly in good weather. Our [Rear Pull-Out Kitchen Pod](/products/rear-pull-out-kitchen-pod) uses heavy-duty runners rated to 220 kg, with over 300 litres of storage inside the pod itself and space for a twin burner hob.

A common pattern is to combine the two — a static interior pod for everyday use, with a pull-out unit at the rear for when you want to cook outside without filling the cabin with cooking smells.

## Hob Compatibility

Most UK campervan kitchen pods are built around a twin burner hob — typically a Smev or Dometic unit, gas-powered, with a glass lid that doubles as additional worktop space when not in use.

Things to check before you order:

- **Is the hob included?** Many pods (including ours) are sold without the hob, so you can choose the model and fuel type that suits you. Check the cutout dimensions on the pod spec sheet to confirm compatibility.
- **Gas locker requirements** — if you're running a gas hob, you'll need a sealed, ventilated gas locker fitted somewhere in the vehicle. This needs to comply with UK gas safety regulations for habitable vehicles.
- **Electric alternatives** — induction hobs are growing in popularity for campervans with decent leisure battery setups, and they avoid the gas locker requirement entirely. Confirm the pod cutout dimensions match your chosen induction unit.

## Materials That Hold Up

Campervan kitchens get hammered. Heat from cooking, moisture from washing up, knocks from kit being loaded in and out, and constant vibration from driving — all of it adds up. The materials you choose matter more than they would in a stationary kitchen.

**Worktops** — solid oak is the standard for premium pods. It's hard-wearing, refinishable, and looks better with age. Our Kitchen Pod Package uses an 18 mm solid oak worktop with integrated LED lighting underneath, which makes evening cooking much easier in a van without overhead lights.

**Carcass** — CNC-cut poplar plywood is lightweight but strong, and resists warping in the humidity changes a van goes through. Cheaper laminated MDF tends to swell and delaminate around the sink and hob over time.

**Hinges and drawers** — Blum soft-close hinges and Blum Legrabox drawer systems (with push-to-open) are an investment, but they make a measurable difference to daily use. Drawers that catch, slam, or rattle on rough roads turn into a constant annoyance.

## Lighting and Power

A kitchen pod that's nice during the day can become genuinely difficult to use after dark if the lighting is wrong. Integrated LED strips under the locker or worktop edge make a big difference — they light the worktop directly without dazzling the rest of the van. The overhead locker on our package has integrated water-resistant LED under-lighting for this reason.

Don't forget USB points and 12 V sockets near the pod — they'll be used constantly for charging, lights, fans, and small appliances.

## Summary

A good campervan kitchen pod is the difference between cooking being a pleasure and cooking being a chore. The key things to get right:

- **Size to your wheelbase** — SWB or LWB, don't compromise on fit
- **Choose your layout** — static, pull-out, or both, based on how you'll cook
- **Pick materials that last** — solid oak worktops, plywood carcasses, quality hinges and runners
- **Plan storage holistically** — pod plus overhead locker plus under-bed storage
- **Light it properly** — integrated LED makes daily use much better
- **Match the rest of the build** — system design beats piecing it together
