If you are converting a van into a campervan, one of the most important decisions you will make is choosing the rear seating. This guide explains what "M1" and ECE R14.09 mean for campervan seating, what the test involves, and what it does not cover.

## What Does M1 Tested Mean?

M1 is a vehicle classification used across the UK and Europe for vehicles designed to carry passengers — specifically, vehicles with no more than eight passenger seats in addition to the driver's seat.

When a campervan seat is described as "M1 certified" or "M1 tested", it usually means the seat belt anchorages have been pull tested to the M1 load requirements of ECE Regulation 14.

[UK government guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/converting-a-van-to-carry-passengers-in-the-rear/converting-a-van-to-carry-passengers-in-the-rear) says there are no specific regulations covering the conversion of vans to carry passengers. It also says additional seats should be fitted securely, and that seat belt anchorage points should be capable of withstanding the high forces of an impact.

## The Standard Behind It: ECE R14.09

ECE Regulation 14 covers seat belt anchorages in motor vehicles. In a pull test to this regulation, loads are applied to the seat belt anchorages to check that they hold.

It is a static test of anchorage strength. It is not a crash test.

## Why Test Evidence Matters for Your Conversion

### Insurance

Government guidance says you should tell your insurance company about the changes you make, the number of passengers and the manner in which they will be carried. Ask your insurer what evidence they need.

### MOT

The [MOT inspection manual](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/mot-inspection-manual-for-private-passenger-and-light-commercial-vehicles/7-other-equipment) requires the tester to inspect the anchorage points of all seat belts fitted, and the condition of the belts. Government guidance says there is no formal checking procedure for private conversions.

### Selling the Vehicle

[DVLA guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/converting-a-vehicle-into-a-motor-caravan/converting-a-vehicle-into-a-motor-caravan) says that if you sell the vehicle at a later date, it is your responsibility as the registered keeper to demonstrate the conversion work has been safely completed. Keep your test documents and installation records.

### Passenger Safety

Seat belts only work if their anchorages hold. A pull test shows that the anchorages on the tested seat held the required loads on the test structure. It does not assess the vehicle floor the seat is fixed to, or the installation itself. Those depend on the vehicle and the installer.

## What to Ask a Supplier

If a manufacturer claims M1 certification, ask for the test document. It should reference the regulation, identify the test house and the specific product that was tested, and say how the seat was fixed for the test. Be cautious of vague claims like "crash tested" or "meets safety standards" without documents.
